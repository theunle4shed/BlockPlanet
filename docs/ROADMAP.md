# BlockPlanet roadmap

A living plan. Each item lists what it involves, what it depends on, and how big it is. Sizes are rough: **S** is days, **M** is one to three weeks, **L** is one to two months, **XL** is a multi-month project. All sizes assume part-time work, testing on a real PC, and that the item's research does not turn up something unexpected.

The longer-term design direction (visual identity, camera, story, modes, junk rules, media player, accessibility) is in [REDESIGN.md](REDESIGN.md). This roadmap covers what to build and in what order.

## How the game is built, and why it matters for these plans

BlockPlanet runs the original game code, recompiled to PC code, inside N64ModernRuntime, with RT64 drawing the graphics. That shapes every item below:

- **Host-side changes** (menus, audio output, screen size, presentation, networking) are ordinary C++ and comparatively easy.
- **Game-side changes** (new magic, camera, HUD layout, sphere behavior) mean finding the relevant original functions and data, then hooking or replacing them. Blocksphere already built hooking tools for its widescreen HUD (`config/recomp/ui_anchor_callsites.json`, `tools/recomp/ui_anchor_hooks.py`). Each game-side item starts with a research step, and the research decides the real size.
- **Art** (HUD, menus, characters, visualizers) needs someone to make it. Texture replacement in RT64 means new art can be swapped in without touching game code.

## Phase 1: quick wins

### Quality and performance modes (M, after Phase 0 measurements)

Presets in Options that turn several dials together, so the game scales from handhelds to high-end PCs. Each dial is measured with `--perf-log` on real hardware before defaults are chosen.

| Dial | Performance | Quality |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Internal resolution | native or 2× | 4K class |
| MSAA | off | 4× or 8× |
| Sphere detail (see below) | Original or Extended | Extended or Full |
| Framebuffer readback | deferred | full |
| Frame rate target | 60, or 30 on weak handhelds | 60, 120 and above |
| Background and visualizer effects | simple | full |
| Texture filtering | basic | enhanced |

**Sphere detail** has three steps, because the game draws a fixed patch of the sphere around the view (see [blockplanet-game-state.md](research/blockplanet-game-state.md)):
1. **Original:** the N64 patch. Cheapest, but blocks go missing near the edge of the view.
2. **Extended:** covers the whole visible half of the sphere and fixes the edge problems. Available as the SPHERE graphics setting (two extra cells on every side), and the default.
3. **Full:** every block with an exposed side, which the rear or peek camera needs. This step also makes the far side visible through an opened core: the undersides of far-side blocks, darkened so they read as distant and in shadow. The original never draws them.

The larger steps cost mostly CPU (the game builds the vertices itself) and buffer memory rather than GPU time, so they may matter more on handhelds than on desktop PCs. Where Full is too expensive, the rear camera can fall back to a simpler view rather than being removed.

**Handhelds:** Steam Deck and Windows handhelds run the PC build, so they need tuned presets rather than a port. Android would need the recompiled C built for ARM and the graphics and platform layers ported.

### Spheres not appearing completely or fast enough (research S, fix S to M)

Large multi-layer spheres seem to build in over several frames when they come into view. The likely cause is a per-frame work budget that made sense on N64 hardware. Plan: capture it happening, find the code that builds sphere geometry and any per-frame limit, then raise or remove the limit on PC. Blocksphere's 1.0 notes say it did not change sphere size or culling, so this area is untouched.

## Phase 2: presentation

### Variable screen sizes: ultrawide, vertical and any window shape (L)

- **Wider than 16:9 (21:9, 32:9): done as the Picture setting's Fill window** (now the default): RT64's expand mode follows the window's shape, and Blocksphere's HUD anchors keep the HUD at the edges. Checked at 21:9, 32:9 and 16:10 on the title, in play and in the pause; other scenes still to be looked at on a real ultrawide screen.
- **Taller than 4:3 (portrait or phone-like):** RT64 widens rather than heightens, so this needs a vertical field-of-view adjustment and new HUD anchoring for top and bottom edges. The sphere then moves closer (camera distance, player context `+0x2420`, × 4:3 ÷ the window's shape) so it keeps the original's share of the narrow side.
- **Free resizing:** pick the projection from the actual window shape instead of fixed presets.

Depends on: research on the game's background layer, which is a fixed 320×240 image drawn as 40 tiles and will need extending or replacing at other aspect ratios. Do this together with the HUD redesign.

### Modern HUD and graphics (L, plus art)

Redesign the in-game HUD (score, hearts, magic, level and character portraits) with modern art that scales to any screen size. Two layers:
1. Replace the HUD textures through RT64 texture packs (art only, no code).
2. Change HUD layout and anchoring through the existing UI hooks so elements sit sensibly at every aspect ratio.

Depends on: the screen-size work, and an art direction decision.

### Modern menus and options screens (L, plus art)

Replace the original menus (title, mode select, options, controls, graphics) with new ones on the RmlUi layer. Game menus that change game state (mode select, name entry, save slots) must keep driving the original game logic, so each screen needs a map of which guest functions and memory it uses. Start with the host-only screens (graphics, controls, audio, jukebox), then replace game screens one by one.

**Secret codes become menu items.** The original unlocks extras by typing special names under NEW NAME: LINES (a hidden mode), CREDITS, G + alien icon + MEBOY (special music), and a five-icon sequence (planet, ship, rocket, heart, skull) for level select, as recorded in the 1997 guide cited in [modes-matrix.md](research/modes-matrix.md). The new menus offer these directly: Lines in the Single mode list, Credits as a menu entry, level select (below), and the special music in the jukebox. Research: find what each name sets in game state, so a menu item can do the same without the code.

**Level select by chapter.** Level select appears once a player has completed a chapter and offers every chapter that player has completed. Research: how the game records each player's progress in its save data (levels are numbered chapter:level, for example 1:1).

Direction: a modern, simple, TV-like menu (large focus states, few items per screen, quick transitions), with no option to keep the original menus.

**Idea: toasts (2026-11-10, not built).** A short note over the menus that fades in and out by itself after a few seconds, with nothing to dismiss, in the menus' note colour, for passing information such as "Playing as Guest. Progress isn't saved." A version was built and withdrawn the same day (the owner left guest play as it was); it could return when a screen needs one.

**Language, starting with English (UK) and English (US) (idea, the owner's, 2026-11-11).** BlockPlanet's text is written in British English (Colour, Centred, Grey). A Language option would offer English (UK) and English (US) first (Colour/Color, Centre/Center, Grey/Gray, Favourite/Favorite, Customise/Customize, Licence/License), defaulting to the system's region, and make room for other languages later. Needs: every menu, card and HUD string moved out of the screens (`assets/ui/screens/*.rml`) and the C++ lists into a string table per language, with English (UK) as the source; the text rewrites for the game's own lines (`proper_case`, `fix_wording`) run per language too; the on-screen keyboard gets a layout per language. Nothing from the game's text is stored (it stays read from the player's copy); only BlockPlanet's own text is translated.

## Phase 3: audio and visuals

### How a stage's sphere is built (research session, M; the owner, 2026-11-18)

The question: is there one seed for the whole sphere, one per layer, or is each block chosen at random as it is placed? Known so far (docs/research/blockplanet-level-data.md, blockplanet-game-state.md): each stage has a settings record (layers, colours, the core square's size, special blocks), not a stored design; the core layer is a fixed pattern; the playfield layers are random on every start (two starts of chapter 1 differed in 887 of 1,024 cells of layer 1).

**Found (2026-11-18, [research](research/blockplanet-sphere-generation.md)):** one sphere, one seed. At a stage's start the game draws a 16-bit sphere seed and restarts its generator (`0x800E2AA0`, `func_80081E90`) from it for each player, so both players get the same sphere; player 1's piece seed (`0x801125BC`) is the second input. Forcing both gives the same sphere every time. Not one seed per layer: layers are built bottom up from one stream, each by about 2,048 random piece drops, then their gaps are filled with single blocks. Each mode loads its own settings record (layers, piece kinds and more) from one table; Training and Puzzle use stored layouts. Research switches can force the seeds and write hand-made layers.

Next, if wanted: shared stage codes (chapter, stage and the two seeds), a daily stage, a layout editor for designed spheres (the piece shapes' part numbering still to map), and edited settings records for unusual stages.

**Experiment: take away the piece balancing (the owner, 2026-11-19; for later).** The piece generator does not pick a kind outright: a draw picks one of 8 slots, and each slot hands out the kinds in turn (`func_8008A63C`, slots staggered by `func_8008A5F0`), so with two kinds one is never more than 4 pieces ahead of the other and never more than 8 come in a row ([sphere generation](research/blockplanet-sphere-generation.md)). A hook there could pick the kind straight from the draw instead (or weight it), to see what spheres come out: long runs and patches of one kind, layers lopsided towards one colour, more or fewer filler blocks. The same generator also hands out the Next pieces in play, so it changes play too (long runs of one piece), not only the sphere. If the results are fun, it could become a stage option or a mode of its own (a "wild" sphere), and with a seed it works for the Daily Challenge as well. To try with both seeds forced, so the balanced and unbalanced builds of one stage can be compared. **Tried (2026-11-19, [results](research/blockplanet-sphere-generation.md)):** without the balancing the sphere looks much the same (the builder's fitting decides more than the order), but play changes (up to 14 of one piece in a row instead of 7); a bias (`bias:P`, 75 % tried) makes properly odd spheres, mostly one colour. Ideas from it: a "streaky" option (no balancing) and a "lopsided" sphere (a bias), both seedable. **Decided (the owner, 2026-11-19): for later, with the customisations** (the custom rulesets below), not as settings now. To settle then: where they are chosen (a custom game's rules, or the start card) and how they treat records and trophies (kept apart from normal runs, or not counted). The research switch `BLOCKPLANET_PIECE_BALANCE` already does both.

**What it means for the plans (read through 2026-11-18, the owner's experiments in that session):**
- **Seeded stages and the Daily Challenge:** solved in principle. A stage is chapter, stage and two 16-bit seeds (the sphere seed and player 1's piece seed); forced, the sphere is identical every time, and the piece seed runs on into play, so it very likely fixes the pieces handed out too (to compare piece by piece before relying on it for fair times). A daily stage takes both seeds from the date. A stage code is those numbers, about eight letters. Two players in one game already get identical spheres.
- **Settings records make new stage types without new code:** byte 0 layers (up to 7: see the ceiling), byte 2 how many piece kinds and byte 11 which (1 to 6 tested; the Next pieces follow the same setting), byte 9 the core square. A Custom stage menu (layers, kinds, core size, seed) is all known values.
- **Designed spheres:** a layer written by hand plays as the game's own (`BLOCKPLANET_SPHERE_LAYOUT`); single blocks, holes and special cells go anywhere, pieces need their shape's parts placed. That is the way to Escape's shell boards and Multiphasic's next sphere, and to a layer editor.
- **The ceiling:** the board is 9 layers and blocks cannot be written in the top one, so a sphere of 7 layers leaves no room for a drop. Letting pieces rest in layer 8 is about nine small hooks (one more layer: a playable 7-layer "Deep" variant); a taller board is large. Multiphasic and Escape must fit within it.
- **New pieces:** within 3 × 3 and up to 9 cells, the game's own placing, overlap and landing code works unchanged, and a whole shape record can be generated from its layout. A five-cell cross was built and played (replacing the T, as all seven materials the game accepts are used). Its combo rule is a choice: the owner's idea of ends only, tip to tip as the bars work, made no combos on a random sphere (it suits designed stages), while any touching position played normally. Still needed for a real new piece: its own colour (widening the material checks), a Next picture and its own shape ID. The research suggests offering it in an Extras mode, not in Rescue.
- **Other ideas from the research** (its "Anatomy of a Sphere" page, private until shared): a turn button that swaps the bar in hand between flat and standing (its twin kind, so every check stays right; the game has no rotation); a longer Next panel as an optional help (the game knows eight pieces ahead); easy and hard versions of a stage by measuring seeds; a pattern per piece kind for colour blindness (each kind is one fixed shape and material); per-layer seeds.
- **For the community:** the research note is written to be shared when the game is out (the owner, 2026-11-18). The generator's re-implementation is kept out of the repository until the owner decides.

### Background animations: understand them (research M)

Gameplay frames render an off-screen background layer at RDRAM `0x000400` with two projections, which the main frame then draws as forty 64×30 tiles. Research questions:
- Which code generates each background and its animation parameters (speed, color, geometry)?
- Are the backgrounds procedural, or stored animations?
- How much of each frame's GPU time do they cost?

**Found so far (2026-11-10, [research](research/blockplanet-play-backgrounds.md)):** each is a stored animation: a LightWave scene with one object, keyframed, played and looped in real time by the game's general model player. There are 25, picked per level by the mode's rule. They are not linked to the music. Only the mesh moves: one small textured mesh (one 32 × 32 texture), turned by its keyframes and shaded by the game's lights, which give the colour shifts. No textures scroll and no palettes cycle (`BLOCKPLANET_BG_DL_LOG`). Still open: the GPU cost.

### Music-reactive backgrounds, like a Winamp visualizer (M to L, after research)

Analyze the audio the game outputs (BlockPlanet already receives every PCM buffer in `queue_audio`) with an FFT to get bass, mid and treble levels and beat detection. Then either:
- **Drive the original backgrounds:** write those levels into the background's animation parameters each frame, if the research finds suitable ones. Original look, music-reactive.
- **Add new backgrounds:** draw host-side shader backgrounds into the background layer. Fully new visuals, the most freedom, and the basis for user-made visualizers later.

### A TV edition for Samsung smart TVs (idea, not a priority, 2026-11-14)

The owner's idea: a relaxed version that runs on the TV itself (the owner's UE75TU7020, Samsung's 2020 4K range, Tizen 5.5), not streamed from a PC, played with the TV's remote or a controller paired with it, with simplified graphics and only casual modes without time limits.
- **What it would be:** a new game of its own, as with the SNES idea: BlockPlanet runs the original game's code with a Vulkan/Direct3D renderer, which a TV app cannot have, and Tetrisphere itself is not ours to put on a TV. BlockPlanet's own designs fit: **Minefield on the sphere** first, and a casual sphere-digging mode in the spirit of Rescue without timers or lives.
- **How:** a Tizen web app (HTML5 with WebGL), the way the owner's CurioCat is built: a simple, flat-shaded sphere of cubes, few effects, large text for a 75-inch screen across a room, and navigation by the remote's arrows and OK (a paired controller as well).
- **The game's look, from the owner's own ROM (2026-11-14):** for the owner's own TV only, installed in developer mode (not for Samsung's store). As with BlockPlanet on PC, nothing from the game is in the app: on first start it reads the owner's ROM backup from the USB drive in the TV and takes the bricks' textures and colours, the core, and perhaps the music from it, while the rules are BlockPlanet's own, simple code. Running the game's own code is out: the TV cannot compile it as the PC does, and too slow to emulate an N64.
- **What to check first, on the TV:** WebGL speed with a sphere of a few hundred cubes, whether the app may read the USB drive (a filesystem permission that 2020 TVs may restrict), and how the remote's keys arrive.
- **Not:** a port of BlockPlanet, or streaming (Steam Link or Moonlight from the PC remains the way to play BlockPlanet itself on the TV).

### A SNES version of Minefield on the sphere, with the Super FX 2 (idea, saved for later, 2026-11-13)

The owner's question: could the game be scaled down for the SNES and its Super FX 2 chip? Not as a port: BlockPlanet runs the original N64 game's own code, which cannot go to a SNES, and Tetrisphere itself (name, look, levels) is not ours to remake. But BlockPlanet's own design, **Minefield on the sphere**, could be a new SNES game of its own, written from scratch as a separate project.
- **What the hardware allows:** the Super FX 2 (21 MHz) draws flat-shaded polygons into a smaller window (as Star Fox 2 and Doom did): about 224 x 160 or less, perhaps 15 to 20 frames a second, a few hundred simple polygons a frame. The visible sphere (about 15 x 15 cells of cubes, 300 to 600 faces) is at that limit, so: flat colours, no textures, simple lighting. A cheaper alternative needs no Super FX at all: the sphere pre-drawn at each turn angle as sprites.
- **What suits the SNES well:** the rules, the numbers on the core, the menus.
- **First step:** a proof of concept in an emulator (bsnes, Mesen): a flat-shaded turning sphere of cubes with the Minefield rules, to see whether the frame rate feels right. Then the arenas, flags, the HUD and sound.
- **Effort:** months, in 65816 assembly plus Super FX code with the SNES homebrew tools. On real hardware it needs a Super FX donor board or a flash cart that supports the chip (such as the FXPak Pro).

### Seeded stages for time trials (idea, saved for later, 2026-11-06)

A **seed number** for each level, so the same stage can be replayed exactly and timed fairly, level by level as well as by episode: shareable seeds, a "daily stage", personal and (later) online bests per seed. Today every level's board is generated randomly at each start ([level data](research/blockplanet-level-data.md); two starts of 1:1 differed in 887 of 1,024 cells), and the piece queue is random too ([piece queue](research/blockplanet-game-state.md#the-piece-queue-2026-11-05)), so stage times are only comparable per episode. To find out: where the game's random number generator lives and how it is seeded; whether seeding it at a level's start reproduces both the board and the piece sequence (the queue keeps eight pieces ahead, so the sequence must come from the same seeded source); what else draws from it during play (effects, magic, the CPU in VS), which could make play diverge even with the same seed; and how a seed is shown and entered (the level select, the records file). Puzzle's boards are already fixed, so its bests are fair as they are.

**The Daily Challenge (the owner, 2026-11-18).** Every day the game picks a new seed, and everyone playing that day gets the same sphere and pieces, with an online leaderboard for score and for time.
- **The seed needs no server:** it can come from the date itself (the day in UTC, hashed), so every copy of the game picks the same one, even offline, and a day's seed can be replayed later.
- **The leaderboard does need one:** a small online service that takes a run (profile name, the day, score, time) and returns the day's table. To decide: where it is hosted and who runs it; what a player's name is (a display name chosen for it, not the profile's real details); and cheating, which on PC cannot be stopped entirely. A run could send its seed and inputs so a result can be checked by replaying it, which needs play to be fully repeatable from the seed (the sphere research session is finding this out).
- **Offline first:** the daily stage with a personal best per day works without any of that. **Online comes last** (the owner, 2026-11-18): the leaderboard, and anything else online, is the last thing worked on, after everything else.
- Fits with Multiphasic and Endless Score Attack (a daily run of either), and with Time Attack's splits.
- **A daily Lines stage too (the owner, 2026-11-19):** Lines uses the same two seeds (sphere and pieces), so a daily seed works there the same way, and its simpler rules suit a short daily competition. Before it is used for fair competition: compare the pieces handed out piece by piece in two runs with the same seeds, and find anything else in play that draws from the random number generator (magic, effects, the "?" wildcard piece), which could make two runs of the same seed differ.

### New game modes (ideas, saved for later, 2026-11-05)

**Adventure (idea, the owner's, 2026-11-11).** One journey on a map of stages drawn on a planet or sphere, each stage its own game type. - **Points and unlocks:** medals convert to points, which unlock further stages on the map. Some small side branches open only on a gold.
- **Independent stages:** once unlocked, any stage can be played at any time, in any order. Lives come from each stage's own configuration, not from a run.
- **Themes as rewards:** stages can come with their own theme (sphere look and background), and finishing them unlocks that theme for use anywhere. Example, Neutron: bright blue brick borders, dark grey bricks, the selected bricks lit in neon too, and a matching background. See the themes note in [REDESIGN.md](REDESIGN.md) §12.
- **Hidden modes found on the map** (the owner's idea, 2026-11-12): some modes are not in the menus at first but are discovered in Adventure, for example **Minefield**: a hidden stage on the map plays it, and finding it unlocks the mode for Solo.
- **Story stays as it is,** perhaps renamed (for example "Classic") so the two are told apart.

**Minefield (the owner's idea, 2026-11-11; a flat prototype is in Solo, marked test).** BlockPlanet's own take on the classic hidden-mines puzzle: a grid hides mines, uncovering a cell shows how many of its eight neighbours hold one (an empty cell opens its neighbours), flags mark the mines, and uncovering a mine ends the game. The first cell uncovered and its neighbours are always safe; on a number with its mines flagged, A uncovers the rest around it. Three sizes: Small (9 × 9, 10 mines), Medium (12 × 12, 24), Large (16 × 16, 40). The rules (`tetrisphere/minefield.h`) already allow a grid that wraps at its edges, for a later version on the sphere itself (its 32 × 32 board wraps both ways), drawn with BlockPlanet's sphere and bricks. Still to decide: a timer and best times, hearts instead of instant loss, and how it fits Adventure.

**Minefield on the sphere (the owner's design, 2026-11-11).** The flat prototype plays well; the real mode is on the sphere:
- **The field:** up to 32 × 32 (the game's board, which wraps both ways, so a full field has no edges and every cell eight neighbours). Custom arena shapes too (the owner's idea): a cross whose two bands each wrap right round the sphere, rings, holes. The rules need a playable mask for this (walls are never mines and count as no neighbour), with wrapping kept across the gaps.

- **Play:** the drop button uncovers the cube under the cursor (it disappears; animation to decide), showing the number on the core beneath or a mine; a mine flashes and explodes (effect to decide). The slide button places and removes flags. The first cube uncovered is safe.

The core's look comes from its tile values (layer 0; the owner's hint that Hide & Seek's core differs): 90 to 93 are Story 1:1's glowing core, 72 to 80 the picture tiles, 81 to 89 a plain flat white core, now used. The owner chose the gold core seen in that test (18 to 26), but laying those values from the start, or just after a cell opens, leaves the core undrawn (black): what made it gold in the test is still to find; the plain core stays meanwhile. **Multiphasic.** Several levels stitched together seamlessly through visual trickery. It starts with stage 1, which looks the same as usual: dig down to the core. First technical notes (not yet researched):

- **Escape:** the camera inside the sphere means the renderer drawing the inner faces and culling differently (BlockPlanet now draws the sphere itself, `docs/research/blockplanet-game-state.md`). Dropping onto the underside reverses the drop direction and gravity, which is game logic spread over many functions (the board limits table in the same document); likely the largest change of the two.
- **Escape, step 1 (2026-11-10, the owner's go): a visual prototype**, `BLOCKPLANET_ESCAPE_VIEW=1`, off by default. The rules don't change.
  - **The bricks' undersides are modelled by the game.** Its sphere code has an inner (core-facing) face routine, `func_8009C6D4`, the same quad as the outer face on the block's inner corners, wound the other way, with its shading negated and its own texture coordinates. The game never calls it: its quadrant passes draw only the outer faces and the sides.

So the bricks are the board's real ones: their texture, shapes, filler blocks and colours, as the outside view shows them. Only the viewpoint and the face change; nothing is BlockPlanet's own layout.
  - The core is not drawn, and a cell dug down to it is open.
  - Its camera is pulled back towards the far side's inner surface, looking across at the board (the owner's framing; `BLOCKPLANET_ESCAPE_DISTANCE`, 0.85 by default). It has a 70° view (`BLOCKPLANET_ESCAPE_FOV`).
  - The game's cursor, falling piece and magic are still drawn from outside, which is the point of the prototype.
  - **Checked on the real game** (Story 1:1): the real bricks read well from below, the core never shows, and at 150° the view becomes a tunnel. The cursor, falling piece and landing marker are missing (drawn from outside), turning barely shows, and a stray quad appeared after a drop.

  - Still to find on the game: the landing routine, the miss test and gravity (three write watches).

  - **Escape, step 3 (2026-11-10): the code read statically.**

## Phase 5: online multiplayer (XL)

**Cooperative** (two players, one sphere, drop and support roles) is specified in [REDESIGN.md](REDESIGN.md#8-cooperative-new). Local Cooperative can come before online play; it depends on the sphere mapping from the culling research and on the magic research.

Online play for up to 4 players, where each player's actions affect the other players' spheres.

- **The original game supports 2-player split-screen VS only.** Four spheres on one screen would be a large rewrite. The practical design is that **each player runs their own single-player game**, and BlockPlanet sends attacks between games over the network, using the attack mechanics VS already has where possible.
- **Networking:** a small relay or lobby server, room codes, and a message protocol for attacks, scores and player status. This is a more forgiving design than lockstep netplay, which would need fully deterministic emulation that the current runtime does not provide.
- **Research first:** how VS sends attacks between the two players, and whether that path can take attacks from a network message.
- **Other costs:** hosting the server, cheating, NAT traversal, and a lobby UI.

A sensible first milestone is two players online with VS-style attacks, then four.

**Host progress in online games.** Guests can play any stage the host has unlocked. Playing it does not count towards the guest's own completion, unlocks or trophies.

**Real lighting and shadows (nice to have, once everything else is done; idea 2026-11-07).** The game's lights are N64 lights: a few directional lights and an ambient level, worked out per vertex, so sides facing a light brighten but nothing casts shadows. ## Decisions

- **Platform:** Windows first. The code already builds and runs on Linux, so Linux and Steam Deck releases can follow later as packaging and testing work.
- **HUD art:** made by the project owner. The current HUD stays until the new art is ready. [HUD_INVENTORY.md](HUD_INVENTORY.md) lists what the HUD shows, where and when.
- **Look:** modern only, with no "Classic" option. Menus should be simple and TV-like, with quick transitions.
- **Online:** private rooms joined with a friend code. No public matchmaking.
- **Saves:** kept in one folder per PC, shared by every Windows account (`C:\ProgramData\BlockPlanet`), with a portable option; several player profiles, each with its own save, trophies and progress, plus guest play. A save file per profile holds progress and preferences; records (best times, scores) are a separate file shared by the PC. Controls belong to the profile. Profiles are chosen, created or deleted at launch, and per player in local multiplayer. See [SAVES.md](SAVES.md).
- **Order:** get the modes working as designed (see [REDESIGN.md](REDESIGN.md)) before extending trophies and statistics; the events they need (combos, blocks destroyed, magic, junk, battle results) come with that work.
- **Profile characters:** each profile picks a character to represent it, from the characters rescued in the game (with optional colour variants), with more added later.
- **Board:** the board stays 32 × 32 cells with 9 layers, as in the game. Its limits are built into about 70 game functions (see [blockplanet-game-state.md](research/blockplanet-game-state.md#layers-core-size-and-stacking-2026-11-02)). Sphere changes are visual only: drawing more of it, and possibly a different core size.
- **Story game over:** kept as the original: unlimited Try Agains, each restarting the chapter from its first level with full hearts and the score reset (tested: 11 retries in a row, never refused). Every mode with chapters whose game over goes back to the chapter's first level (Story, Hide & Seek) offers Restart chapter in its pause menu, after a confirmation, rather than Restart level; other modes offer Restart level: getting through a chapter without a game over is the challenge.
- **Sphere framing:** as close to the original as possible. The sphere keeps the original's size relative to the screen's narrow side at rest, its growth with stage depth (about 55% of the height at 1:1, larger than the screen at 10:1) and the drop-timer zoom (up to about twice the size). The camera rests at the same distance at every stage (confirmed at 1:1 and 10:1), so deep stages may overflow the screen at rest, as in the original. With Fill window this already holds for every screen 4:3 or wider; portrait and squarish windows will get it with the portrait work, by moving the camera (player context `+0x2420`) closer by 4:3 ÷ the window's shape. No Sphere size setting.
