# What Tetrisphere players say: wishes, complaints, bugs and what they love

Research date: 2026-11-09. Purpose: find what players and critics say about the original Tetrisphere (Nintendo 64, 1997, H2O Entertainment), so BlockPlanet can be its best version. The last section compares the findings with [ROADMAP.md](../ROADMAP.md), [REDESIGN.md](../REDESIGN.md) and [CHANGES-SINCE-BLOCKSPHERE.md](../../CHANGES-SINCE-BLOCKSPHERE.md).

## How this was gathered, and its limits

- **Method:** web searches across reviews (1997 and modern), wikis, forums, emulator issue trackers and interviews. Only short player quotes are given; no game text is copied.
- **Limit: pages could not be opened directly.** This session's network policy blocked fetching pages (tcrf.net, Wikipedia, TASVideos, GameFAQs and others all refused), so every finding comes from the search engine's summaries of those pages. Quotes are as the search results gave them. Where a summary was thin or uncertain, it is marked *unverified*. Reading the linked pages directly is the obvious next step.
- **Limit: no Reddit.** The search tool would not return reddit.com, and Reddit pages could not be fetched, so community opinion here comes mainly from GameFAQs (reviews and boards), ResetEra, the Nintendo 64 Forever forum, Steam, review sites, retro blogs and emulator issue trackers. speedrun.com could not be reached either; no Tetrisphere leaderboard turned up in searches (*unverified*).
- **How often:** "widely" means it comes up in many independent sources; "often" in several; "sometimes" in two or three; "once" in a single source.

## Summary: the top suggestions

1. **Make the rules easier to learn.** The single most repeated point, from reviewers to Tetris' own creator: the game is great once understood but hard to grasp. BlockPlanet's Training rework, How to Play and rules cards are the right direction; keep pushing (short interactive lessons, clear feedback on why a drop failed).
2. **Make the cursor and controls feel precise.** The second most common complaint: the cursor is hard to read (can a piece drop here? is it locked on?) and controls feel less responsive than wanted. Clear drop/no-drop feedback, analog stick support and a tunable cursor speed would answer it.
3. **Let players see the whole sphere.** Pop-in at the edges and hidden blocks on the far side were criticised in 1997 and still are; the Tetris Company's Henk Rogers calls hiding pieces the game's cardinal sin. BlockPlanet's Extended sphere already fixes the edges; the peek camera and see-through core answer the rest.
4. **Treat the music as a headline feature.** The soundtrack is the most praised thing about the game, and fans note it was never officially released. A jukebox, track names and proper music choice are high-value and already planned.
5. **Keep the core rules and the feel of big combos.** Players love the "bubble wrap" chain reactions, the originality and the hypnotic presentation. Do not simplify the rules themselves; make them easier to learn.
6. **More reasons to keep playing.** Some critics found it repetitive or lacking the endless pull of Tetris. Endless Score Attack, seeds, daily stages and trophies (planned) address this.
7. **Multiplayer is valued but small.** Two-player VS is praised as the N64's first competitive puzzle game; local two-player in BlockPlanet is "coming later", so it should not slip far.
8. **Fix the known original quirks where cheap:** the European music-list glitch and missing track (see bugs), and seams or lines that emulators show.

Few player-reported bugs turned up. The game seems stable on real hardware; most reports concern regional differences and emulators.

## 6. Compared with BlockPlanet's plans

### Already done

- **Controls:** left stick and D-pad, remapping by action, prompts for each device (answers "no analog stick").
- **Learning curve:** Training reworked into the menus, a first-time "Start with Training" choice, How to Play per mode, rewritten cards with one glossary of terms, the player's own button symbols in the text.
- **Edge pop-in:** the Extended sphere (the default) draws the whole visible half and fixes blocks going missing at the edges, the 1997 Next Generation complaint.
- **Readable HUD:** BlockPlanet's own HUD in Story, Hide & Seek, Puzzle, Lines and VS CPU, with a combo counter (answers GameSpot's 1997 point).
- **Secrets as menu items:** Lines available from the start; Credits on the main menu.
- **Relaxed play:** `--casual` (no game over).
- **Modern screens:** ultrawide, crash reports, separate music and effects volume, profiles and backed-up saves.

### Planned (the research supports the priority)

- **Peek camera, Full sphere detail and the see-through core** answer the hidden-pieces complaint, the one Henk Rogers singles out. Fans and critics both name it; it deserves its place in "single player is finished when".
- **Sphere pop-in when large spheres build in** (Phase 1) matches the 1997 pop-up complaint as well.
- **Jukebox, track names, special music:** the soundtrack is the most loved feature, so the jukebox is a high-value quick win.
- **Mouse and keyboard controls,** accessibility (colour-blind palettes, fall speed, assists), level select by chapter.
- **Endless Score Attack, Multiphasic, seeds, daily stage, trophies:** answer "repetitive" and "lacks the endlessness of Tetris".
- **Local two-player VS** ("coming later") and online play: two-player VS is part of what critics praised.

### New from this research (not yet in the roadmap)

1. **Cursor clarity.** Make it unmistakable whether a drop is allowed, and whether the cursor is on a piece for sliding (outline, colour and a short sound), and show why a drop was refused. The cursor is the second most common complaint.
2. **Input responsiveness.** Check the TASVideos claim that input is handled only about every 5 frames. If so, consider reading input every frame on the host side for cursor movement only, without changing simulation speed or timers (AGENTS.md). Measure first.
3. **Cursor speed and drag strength.** The original has Speed and Power settings for the cursor (the manual); make sure BlockPlanet's Options expose them clearly.
4. **Region support.** If European ROMs are supported: the missing track and the music-list glitch (Choose mode wrapping onto the title theme) are easy, safe fixes in BlockPlanet's own jukebox and music choice. Project64's trouble with the PAL ROM suggests its timing follows 50 Hz output; check how the runtime paces a PAL ROM before claiming support.
5. **Lines multiplier check.** Look at the reported Lines glitch that pushes the multiplier past 20x before Lines records and trophies arrive: decide whether to keep it (a known trick) or cap it, and make records consistent either way.
6. **Rendering traps emulators hit.** The emulator reports list what to test in BlockPlanet's RT64 path: seams between texture rectangles (menus, HUD, intro), piece colours always consistent, 2D title sprites, and fog at the start of Rescue.
7. **VORTEX.** The roadmap lists LINES, CREDITS, MEBOY and level select as name codes to become menu items, but not VORTEX (an animation); add it, perhaps to the jukebox or as a visualiser.
8. **Keep the original Time Trial available somewhere.** It was replaced by Time Attack and Score Attack on Story's episodes; TAS runners and some players know the original Time Trial. Not a strong demand in what was found, so low priority.
9. **Practice settings.** The original Practice mode (pieces, layers, core area, no time limit) is folded into Custom later; keep it reachable, since it is the natural "learn at your own pace" space players ask for.
10. **Rescue variety.** "A little vanilla" (Destructoid) and "repetitive" suggest that new modes and rule presets (Multiphasic, rulesets) matter for long-term play, not only for multiplayer.
11. **Debug strings as research clues.** The `generatelevel.c` and `drop.c` messages in the ROM can help map level generation and drop validation (for seeds and custom levels). Read them from the player's ROM at research time only; do not commit them.

### Things to keep as they are

- The core rules (dropping, sliding, combos, magic, the core goal), the sphere-on-a-fixed-camera feel, and the big chain reactions.
- The soundtrack as the default music.
- A gentle start with Training on first play.
